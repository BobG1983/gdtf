# Pillar 8: Memory is the Moat

**Gangs that remember who crippled whom are the long-game differentiator — and it's data, not art.**

XCOM doesn't do this; Necromunda only gestures at it on paper. A faction that *records* who maimed its leader, who it owes a debt, who it wants dead — and then acts on that memory in future deployments — turns a sequence of unrelated fights into a feud with a history. That is the thing nothing else in the genre delivers.

And it is cheap to build relative to its payoff: a grudge system is rules and a database — the systems-engineering wheelhouse, not engine grind. It generates story for free, on top of fights that already happened. It is the purest expression of [Pillar 1](1-situation-generator.md) and the layer that makes [the roster](2-the-roster-is-the-story.md) into an ongoing saga.

It is **deferred past the MVP** (see [../mvp/campaign.md](../mvp/campaign.md)) — the combat → wound → roster loop has to be proven first — but it is the north star the whole loop is built toward. Don't design the early systems in ways that make grudges hard to bolt on later.
